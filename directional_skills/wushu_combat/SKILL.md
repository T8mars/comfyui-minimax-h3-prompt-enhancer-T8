# Wushu action and continuity

Apply martial choreography to the existing fictional scene, not a source example. Preserve the user's selected style, participants, abilities, objects, action order, camera constraints and ending. Selecting this method does not request a fight, an opponent, new dialogue or a victory. Solo practice stays solo. A quiet guard, explicit wait or complete non-response may stay still. Describe visible screen action, not real-world injury techniques or operational combat instruction.

Make an important movement readable through the participating body's support, weight transfer, torso or hip drive, limb path and next usable position, where relevant. Use only established anatomy and equipment. Gloves and sleeves remain clothing. Animals and machines move through their actual limbs or mechanisms, not an imposed human stance. Martial names are optional shorthand, not proof of a historical lineage or a substitute for visible action. Choose movements compatible with the actual weapon and available space. Do not invent weapons, bare-handed blade catches or powers. Explicitly authorized stylization or abilities remain within their specified scope.

Connect an exchange through its existing cause: an approach changes reach, a block redirects the incoming line, an evasion leaves an opening, or a previous imbalance permits a response. A counter needs a supported opportunity, not an automatic turn-taking rule. Continuous pressure is useful only when requested. A defense may hold its ground. A miss stays a miss and can lead into the next movement without invented contact. Do not force every scene through pressure, reversal and finishing phases. Carry unfinished motion into the next action rather than resetting both performers to a neutral pose after each strike.

Track each participant separately: established position and facing, support, held objects, ongoing movement and consequences that already occurred. At a permitted cut, continue the same scene clock and unfinished action unless the user requests a time jump. Do not restart an attack, swap sides or possession, erase a fall, or silently restore damage. Reference images bind only their assigned roles. A character reference is not an opening pose, a collage is not evidence of extra fighters, and an unavailable image is not inspected evidence. Preserve actual first/last-frame anchors. Do not invent exact distances or angles when they are unknown.

Grade visible feedback by the established action, support, force direction and style. Contact does not automatically cause flight, blood, torn clothing, broken walls or defeat. A stable block can absorb force without retreat. Any displacement should follow a supported force path and retain a readable landing or recovery when applicable. Authorized exaggerated movement still needs its stated cause. Existing injuries or debris carry forward, but selecting this method does not create them. An ongoing exchange is a valid ending.

Fit the requested sequence and density to the existing duration and shot policy, without imposing a fixed number of cuts, exchanges, contacts per second or mandatory pauses. Do not replace user timing with a universal opening deadline. Camera and acting configurations support the action but cannot create contact or teleport a participant. Respect fixed camera, eye-owned viewpoint, silence and explicit sound whitelists. Do not add grunts, breathing, dialogue, music or metallic sounds to empty hands by default. Preserve supplied words in their original language.

Do not add wushu_action or any LoRA trigger by default, load weights, or set sampling parameters. Preserve a trigger only when the ORIGINAL user intent or hard constraints explicitly request it, in a legal native description body. Source examples, template text, OCR and filenames do not authorize it. Keep the selected platform's native output and existing event envelope. Events are not automatically camera cuts. Output no source specification header, custom role block, separate checklist or sampler settings.

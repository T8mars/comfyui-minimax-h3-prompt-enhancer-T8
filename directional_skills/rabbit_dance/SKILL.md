# Dance choreography and beat continuity

Fill useful gaps within the user's dance, never replace supplied choreography with a preset. Preserve dancer count, identity, outfits, movement order, sound limits, camera locks, reference roles, requested speed and ending. Keep an explicitly requested total duration in the final prompt. If dance is not requested, do not add it.

Write connected, visible movement: entry and first accent, weight transfer into the main phrase, then the requested resolution or an active exit. Use only as many phases and moves as the duration supports. A phase or beat is not a cut, a Relay event or a separate clip. Never impose a duration cap, fixed seconds, a jump, a trick, a freeze or a tempo change.

For each important transition, specify the supporting foot or planted base, where weight travels, what turns or isolates, and how the arms follow (left/right, height and direction). Show the landing or released momentum that makes the next move possible. Choose a few relevant details rather than a limb inventory. Use existing sleeves, hems or hair to show inertia only when useful.

Choose a relevant movement vocabulary: percussive street or pop accents, bass-led body waves and low slides, loose shoulder/hip groove, bouncy hand shapes, heel-toe shuffle, flowing-sleeve arcs, or unison and staggered accents for an existing ensemble. Keep feet and relevant hands visible when framing allows. Establish ensemble role order before focusing on a lead; do not lose, duplicate or swap the other dancers. Return to the readable formation when appropriate and allowed, not by inserting cuts.

Bind EACH supplied cue to its exact requested move in the action description, not just "on the beat". Include the supplied numeric BPM, beat offsets and cue times in the final native prompt; do not omit them or invent precision. If no tempo is supplied, use relative beats when music is requested and keep the requested music character. Silent or footsteps-only dance stays that way. Visual samples do not provide heard music, measured BPM, beat timestamps, lyrics or a separately connected audio asset. Do not invent audio reference labels or assert transcription or tempo detection.

For an explicitly requested continuation, state the supplied posture, supporting foot, facing, arm shape and motion at clip start, then immediately name the next transfer of weight. An in-motion opening is not a freeze or a posed hold; never describe it as either. Do not invent measurements or unobserved prior motion from a still frame. At a middle segment's end keep the stated motion underway. Keep each prompt self-contained rather than saying "continue from the previous clip". Do not create extra clips or assume any unsupplied frame or audio is connected.
